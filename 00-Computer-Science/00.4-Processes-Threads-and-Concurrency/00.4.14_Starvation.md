# Module 00.4.14 — Starvation

## What is starvation?
**Starvation** occurs when a thread or process waits indefinitely or for an unreasonably long time because other participants repeatedly receive the resource or execution opportunity it needs.

For example, Thread A repeatedly waits for a lock while other threads acquire and release it. If A never gets access, it is starved. Starvation may result from unfair scheduling, priority policies, or repeated resource allocation to other participants.

## Starvation versus deadlock
- **Starvation:** Other threads may continue making progress, while one thread repeatedly misses out.
- **Deadlock:** The involved threads are stuck waiting on one another and cannot make progress.

In starvation, the resource is not necessarily permanently unavailable; the affected thread simply does not get a fair opportunity.

## Potential mitigations
Depending on the cause, mitigation may involve fairer scheduling or resource-allocation policies, reducing lock hold times, or changing the design to avoid repeated preference for the same participants. Not every lock guarantees fairness.

## AI engineering connection
A backend worker could repeatedly miss access to a shared resource while other workers continue processing. Starvation can cause long-tail latency even when the service as a whole is still doing work.

## Key takeaways
1. Starvation affects participants that cannot get needed CPU time or resources.
2. Other participants can continue making progress.
3. Starvation differs from deadlock, where involved participants are stuck waiting on one another.
4. Fairness depends on scheduling and resource-allocation policy.
