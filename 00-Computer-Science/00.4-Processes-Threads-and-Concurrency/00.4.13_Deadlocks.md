# Module 00.4.13 — Deadlocks

## What is a deadlock?
A **deadlock** occurs when two or more threads or processes are stuck waiting for resources held by one another, so the involved participants cannot make progress.

## Example: circular waiting
- Thread A acquires Lock 1 and waits for Lock 2.
- Thread B acquires Lock 2 and waits for Lock 1.
- A cannot continue until B releases Lock 2.
- B cannot continue until A releases Lock 1.

Neither thread can proceed. The threads may be alive but blocked; a deadlock is not necessarily a CPU-consuming loop. The locks themselves are not literally frozen.

## Common prevention strategies
- **Consistent lock ordering:** Acquire the same set of locks in the same order throughout the program.
- **Timeouts:** Stop waiting after a defined period and handle the failure.
- **Avoid unnecessary nested locks:** Reduce cases where a thread holds one lock while waiting for another.

Timeouts can help recovery but do not automatically resolve every deadlock or guarantee that the overall operation is safe.

## AI engineering connection
Concurrent backend requests may acquire multiple locks to access caches or shared resources. Inconsistent lock ordering can cause workers to stall and requests to stop completing.

## Key takeaways
1. Deadlock involves blocked participants waiting on resources held by one another.
2. A thread can be alive without making progress.
3. Consistent lock ordering is a common prevention technique.
4. Timeouts help bound waiting but require careful error handling.
