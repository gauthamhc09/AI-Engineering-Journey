# Module 00.4.15 — Synchronization

## What is synchronization?
**Synchronization** is the broader concept of coordinating concurrent execution so threads or processes interact safely and in the required order. It is not limited to preventing simultaneous access; it can also coordinate when operations are allowed to proceed.

## Common mechanisms
| Mechanism | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Lock / mutex | Protect a critical section through mutual exclusion |
| Semaphore | Limit how many participants can access a resource concurrently |
| Condition variable | Let threads wait until a condition becomes true |
| Event | Signal that something has happened or a condition is ready |

## Example: document download and processing
Imagine one thread downloads a document while another processes it. The processing thread must not begin until the document is ready.

1. Thread A downloads the document.
2. Thread B waits for a completion signal.
3. Thread A signals that the download is complete.
4. Thread B proceeds to process the document.

An event or another appropriate synchronization primitive can coordinate this dependency; a mutual-exclusion lock is not necessarily the right tool for signaling completion.

## Synchronization versus mutual exclusion
- **Mutual exclusion** prevents multiple participants from entering a protected section simultaneously.
- **Coordination** arranges the order or conditions under which tasks proceed.
- Synchronization is the broader category that includes both.

## AI engineering connection
An AI pipeline may download, validate, and process documents in separate stages. Synchronization ensures that a stage waits for the data or signal it requires rather than processing incomplete input.

## Key takeaways
1. Synchronization coordinates concurrent work.
2. It can protect shared state or establish ordering/dependencies.
3. Different primitives solve different coordination problems.
4. Choose the mechanism based on the required behavior.
